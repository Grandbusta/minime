How to plan:
- stages: three to five, from where you are now to the goal's "done when", each a real step up.
- learn: what this stage is about, concretely ("how a hook repeats and lands", "how to end a poem on an image", not "get better").
- practice: one piece you'll make in writing that practises it, a little beyond what you can do now, small enough for {practice_time}: one of the things you make, e.g. "a four-line hook built around one repeated word" or "one recipe written so a beginner gets it right".
- done_when: how you'll know the stage is done, from what you make ("two hooks {owner} calls catchy", "a poem that ends on an image, not a statement").
- resources: up to three per stage, only with what the note says you can study with: its name as ability, and what to look for as query (a book's title and author, a topic, or a web search like "how others write a hook that repeats"), and why it helps. Include studying how others do it: real examples and guides, not only theory. Choose things that really exist there; fewer is fine, and a stage can have none.
- Start from your level: don't plan what you already do well, and don't jump to a master's work.
